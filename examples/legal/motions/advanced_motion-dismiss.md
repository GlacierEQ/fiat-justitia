# Advanced Exhibit: Multi-Count Motion to Dismiss (Standing, Jurisdiction, Mootness)

**Rule:** Fed. R. Civ. P. 12(b)(1), 12(b)(6)
**Agent Assigned:** strategy-agent
**Proof Class:** filed

## I. Introduction
Defendant moves to dismiss the entirety of Plaintiff's sprawling complaint for lack of subject matter jurisdiction, lack of Article III standing, mootness, and failure to state a claim.

## II. Standard of Review
A Rule 12(b)(1) motion challenges the court's jurisdiction. Without jurisdiction, the court cannot proceed to the merits. Rule 12(b)(6) challenges the legal sufficiency of the claims.

## III. Arguments
**A. Plaintiff Lacks Article III Standing**
Plaintiff has not suffered an injury in fact that is concrete, particularized, and actual or imminent. The alleged harm is speculative and hypothetical.

**B. The Claims are Moot**
Subsequent to the filing of the complaint, the challenged policy was repealed in its entirety, rendering the request for injunctive relief moot. There is no reasonable expectation that the alleged violation will recur.

**C. Failure to Exhaust Administrative Remedies**
Even if jurisdiction existed, Plaintiff failed to exhaust mandatory administrative remedies under the applicable statute before filing suit, constituting a fatal defect to the claims under Rule 12(b)(6).

## IV. Conclusion
Because the Court lacks jurisdiction and the claims are otherwise fatally flawed, the complaint must be dismissed with prejudice.
